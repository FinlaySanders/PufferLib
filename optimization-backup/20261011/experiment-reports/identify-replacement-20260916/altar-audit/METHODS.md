# Altar opportunity, execution and information audit

Frozen additive/replacement 1B policies. Eight rollout seeds per policy, 8,000 decisions each for natural observations. Count altar glyphs in the public map, actual standing-on-altar states, legal ALTAR_ID, eligible unknown-BUC inventory, masked action probability, and selections. Standing is checked against the engine tile and compared to the environment action mask. Public glyph visibility is distinct from a generated altar merely existing somewhere on the level. Counts include partial ongoing episodes and are not performance certificates.

Controlled test (controlled-v2 only): after at least 20 decisions, find healthy/no-condition/not-hungry states with an unknown-BUC item and no hostile within eight tiles. Change a room/corridor tile under the hero into an aligned altar using SET_TYP_XY, which updates both engine terrain representations. Refresh real observations and masks. Paired arms force one ALTAR_ID action or let the policy act normally; follow for 128 decisions including the forced action. Eight contexts per policy. Retain the policy's recurrent history. No items are artificially added: inventory information may or may not be valuable in that context.

The first synthetic fixture changed levl.typ without the engine terrain cache and is INVALID. Root directory mode 1/2 results are retained for debugging but excluded. Root mode 0 never changes terrain and is valid. corrected controlled-v2 updates the cache with the native setter. All paired preparation metadata agree.

Execution checks match object IDs before and after ALTAR_ID: how many previously unknown items are retained, have BUC revealed, or are missing. Log game-turn cost. Worn items are skipped by the action; naturally received additional items can affect the remaining-unknown count. A forced action tests machinery, not learned willingness or benefit.

Information sensitivity: before acting, temporarily reveal BUC via engine bknown for all carried noncoin objects; refresh observations and legal masks; forward from identical pre-action recurrent state. Compare masked verb and item-head distributions using total variation distance. Restore original bknown flags, full observations, original post-forward recurrent state/logits before any real sampling. No random draws in alternate forwards. This tests whether the network/masks react to BUC information, not whether acquiring it improves return. Some changes come from newly valid/invalid actions under known curse state. Subsequent verb/item-BUC choices are logged in controlled-v2, but forced identification consumes turns and changes trajectories, so follow-up differences are not solely information effects.

Limits: a small number of seeds, correlated steps, finite windows, synthetic altars in controlled tests, and naturally held unknown items rather than deliberately valuable identification opportunities. No general never-use claim or proof of why learning failed follows. Production source, model weights, stock NLE and frozen engine library are unchanged; all instrumentation is external.
